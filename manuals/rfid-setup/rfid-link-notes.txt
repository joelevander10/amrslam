RFID READER LINK (LAN1) - AS-BUILT AND HARDENING NOTES
======================================================
Written 2026-08-31. Vehicle profile agv-01.

The RFID reader determines the ROUTE: which tag is seen decides stop, start and
branch. Magnet tape is the road; RFID is the signage. It carries no safety
function - that belongs to the safety lidar, the safety controller and STO - but
a reader that goes silent without being NOTICED means the vehicle drives past its
branch point and never arrives. Everything below exists to make loss of the
reader a fast, LOUD, controlled stop rather than a silent wrong turn.


1. AS-BUILT
-----------
Interface   enp2s0 (LAN1), Intel i225/i226, driver igc, MAC 00:10:f3:b6:f8:01
Host IP     192.168.1.10/24, static
Reader      192.168.1.200, MAC 8c:4b:41:3b:cc:a4, TCP port 2022 open
Topology    direct point-to-point, NO switch
Link        100BASE-TX full duplex, autonegotiated

Config lives in /etc/netplan/60-rfid-lan1.yaml (mode 0600, renderer networkd).
Backup of the pre-change /etc/netplan is at /root/netplan-backup-*.tar.gz.

NOTE on why this is netplan and not nmcli. NetworkManager on this box is
restricted to wifi by /usr/lib/NetworkManager/conf.d/10-globally-managed-devices
.conf, which sets unmanaged-devices=*,except:type:wifi,... Every ethernet port is
therefore invisible to nmcli by design; it belongs to netplan -> systemd-networkd.
Trying to configure enp2s0 with nmcli will appear to do nothing.

Three deliberate choices in that file:

  no gateway4 / routes   The box already carries two default routes (usb0 via
                         10.46.252.34, wlp1s0 via 192.168.2.10). A third default
                         pointing at the reader's subnet would blackhole traffic.
                         The reader is a direct peer; it needs the /24 and
                         nothing else.

  no nameservers         Same reasoning. The reader is addressed by IP forever.

  optional: true         Renders as RequiredForOnline=no. Without it, systemd-
                         networkd-wait-online blocks boot ~2 min whenever the
                         reader is unplugged for maintenance, which reads to an
                         operator as a dead controller.

  renderer: networkd     Scoped INSIDE the ethernets: block, not at the top
                         level. 50-cloud-init.yaml currently declares no global
                         renderer so this is presently redundant - but if cloud-
                         init ever regenerates that file with renderer:
                         NetworkManager, a top-level setting here would fight it
                         and could take wlp1s0 down. Scoped, it cannot.


2. MEASURED BASELINE (2026-08-31, vehicle at standstill)
--------------------------------------------------------
ICMP, 50 packets     rtt min/avg/max/mdev = 0.057/0.096/0.123/0.010 ms, 0% loss
NIC error counters   all zero (no crc, drop, discard, miss, collision)
EEE / green ethernet already disabled - a known link-flap source on igc
Coalescing           rx-usecs 3 (already low latency, do not raise)
TCP connects         30/30 succeed at 0.3 s spacing
Concurrent sockets   reader accepted 3 simultaneous connections - no single-
                     client lock, so a diagnostic tool can attach alongside the
                     controller
Recovery from RST    immediate; reconnect on attempt 1, 0.00 s. The reader does
                     NOT hold a dead session open, so a controller crash does
                     not lock us out.
Port 2022 on connect sends nothing unsolicited -> command/response reader, NOT
                     an auto-streaming one. Something must poll it.

Conclusion: the electrical link is massively over-provisioned for sparse tag
events and is NOT the risk. Do not spend effort on throughput or latency.


3. THE GOVERNING NUMBER
-----------------------
From agv-profile.json: wheel_dia 0.180 m, gear_ratio 30, auto_rpm 1600
  -> wheel circumference 0.5655 m
  -> cruise speed 0.503 m/s (1.81 km/h)

Blind travel at cruise:

  loop tick 20 ms                       0.010 m
  sensor_timeout_s 0.10                 0.050 m   <- existing budget
  line_loss_grace_m 0.075               0.075 m   <- existing budget
  250 ms                                0.126 m
  1 s                                   0.503 m
  30 s                                 15.080 m
  default TCP keepalive 7200 s       3619.115 m

The codebase already budgets blind travel at the 5-7.5 cm scale. A stock TCP
socket would let the vehicle travel 3.6 km past a branch point while still
believing it was connected. Closing that gap is the entire job.


4. PHYSICAL - THE DOMINANT REMAINING RISK
-----------------------------------------
None of this is visible on a bench. Vehicle ethernet fails mechanically.

  * RJ45 latches snap under vibration. Use M12 D-coded if the reader offers it.
    On RJ45, use a latch-protected boot.
  * Use STRANDED conductor cable. Solid core work-hardens and fractures at the
    vibration node.
  * Service loop and strain relief at BOTH ends so cable motion never loads the
    connector itself.
  * Route away from motor power; cross at 90 degrees where unavoidable. This bus
    already runs CAN at 125 kbps for noise-margin reasons - same environment.
  * CHECK THE READER'S POWER FEED. If it shares a rail with the drives, an
    acceleration brownout resets the reader. That failure looks exactly like a
    comms fault and is cured with a separate fused feed, not with software.

KEEP THE LINK DIRECT. No switch. This is a robustness property worth defending:
on a point-to-point link, carrier state is a true proxy for "the reader is
physically there". Insert a switch and a yanked reader-side cable leaves our
carrier up and lying to us.


5. TCP - THE HALF-OPEN PROBLEM
------------------------------
The failure that will actually bite: reader loses power, or the cable parts
mid-run. Our recv() blocks forever. No exception, no error, socket reports
ESTABLISHED indefinitely. See the 3.6 km figure above.

Never rely on defaults. On EVERY connection to the reader:

    s.setsockopt(socket.IPPROTO_TCP, socket.TCP_NODELAY,  1)   # tag events tiny
    s.setsockopt(socket.SOL_SOCKET,  socket.SO_KEEPALIVE, 1)
    s.setsockopt(socket.IPPROTO_TCP, socket.TCP_KEEPIDLE,  1)  # probe after 1 s
    s.setsockopt(socket.IPPROTO_TCP, socket.TCP_KEEPINTVL, 1)
    s.setsockopt(socket.IPPROTO_TCP, socket.TCP_KEEPCNT,   2)  # dead in ~3 s
    s.setsockopt(socket.IPPROTO_TCP, socket.TCP_USER_TIMEOUT, 300)   # ms

Set these PER SOCKET, never via sysctl - a sysctl would drag the wifi and USB
tether sockets along with it and is a system-wide change for a one-link problem.

TCP_USER_TIMEOUT is the one that earns its keep: it bounds how long unacked data
may linger before the socket errors out, regardless of keepalive state.
300 ms is about 15 cm of travel.

FASTER STILL: WATCH CARRIER DIRECTLY. On a direct link,
/sys/class/net/enp2s0/carrier drops to 0 within milliseconds of a cable parting -
far quicker than any TCP mechanism can conclude anything. Treat carrier loss as
an instant fault; let keepalive and TCP_USER_TIMEOUT catch the subtler case where
the cable is fine but the reader firmware has wedged.


6. APPLICATION - TWO DISTINCT FAULTS, NOT ONE
---------------------------------------------
Conflating these two produces a system that either stops constantly or misses
branches. They have different causes and different evidence.

FAULT A - rfid_comms_lost
  Poll or heartbeat the reader continuously; absence of a response within budget
  is a fault. Map it onto the existing sensor_lost path in autopilot.py. The
  reasoning is identical to the comment already there: we no longer know where
  the route markers are, so hold straight and stop - the least-bad blind action.

FAULT B - rfid_tag_overdue
  Comms perfectly healthy, but the reader MISSED a tag - dirt, misalignment, a
  detuned or damaged tag. Odometry says we should have crossed tag N by now and
  we have not. Budget this in DISTANCE, not time, exactly as line_loss_grace_m
  already does, and for the reason given in autopilot.py: distance rescales
  itself with speed instead of needing re-tuning at every step. Keep a time
  ceiling too, for the standstill case where no distance ever accrues.

  Fault B needs a route model - expected tag sequence plus inter-tag distances.
  That is the largest piece of remaining work and it is not a comms problem.

TWO STRUCTURAL RULES, both already established in this codebase for CAN:

  * A DEDICATED RFID THREAD OWNS THE SOCKET. No socket call ever happens on the
    50 Hz tick. The control loop reads a snapshot plus an age, precisely the way
    it consumes sensor_age_s today. A blocking read on the tick would blow the
    20 ms loop budget, and that budget is already tight because of the 125 kbps
    CAN bus.

  * POLL RATE SETS STOP ACCURACY, NOT JUST LIVENESS. At 0.503 m/s, polling at
    5 Hz gives +/-10 cm of uncertainty about WHERE a tag physically was; 50 Hz
    gives +/-1 cm. Because the reader is command/response rather than streaming,
    its sustainable command rate is a hard constraint on stopping precision.
    Measure it once the protocol is known.

Follow the events.py contract: these fire ONCE PER EDGE, never once per poll.
At 50 Hz a per-poll emit flushes the whole 200-entry ring in four seconds and
destroys the record of why the vehicle stopped.


7. OBSERVABILITY
----------------
logs/rfid-link-watch.sh samples at 1 Hz, needs no root, and records carrier,
link speed, ICMP RTT, TCP reachability of :2022 and the summed NIC error
counters. Transitions are marked *** so a flap is greppable.

    nohup /home/gvipc-evo-01/agv_can/logs/rfid-link-watch.sh >/dev/null 2>&1 &
    grep '\*\*\*' /home/gvipc-evo-01/agv_can/logs/rfid-link-watch.log

RUN IT DURING REAL DRIVING. Vibration and EMI faults are invisible at standstill,
and the baseline in section 2 was taken parked - it proves the cable is good, not
that the installation is. Several hours of MOVING soak with zero *** lines is
what justifies trusting this link.
